# INSTITUTIONAL RISK MANAGEMENT & HEDGING (LEVEL: ADMIN)

## 1. Options Strategies for Hedging
Hedging is the practice of taking an offsetting position in a related security to reduce risk.
- Protective Puts: Buying a put option for a stock you already own. This acts as "insurance," allowing you to sell the stock at a pre-set price even if the market crashes.
- Covered Calls: Selling call options on shares you own to generate income. This is a neutral-to-bullish strategy that provides a small cushion against price drops.

## 2. Delta Hedging
Delta hedging is a neutral strategy used by institutional traders to reduce the risk associated with price movements in the underlying asset.
- Mechanism: The trader adjusts their position in the underlying stock based on the 'Delta' (the rate of change of the option price relative to the stock price).
- Goal: To maintain a "Delta Neutral" portfolio where the overall value remains stable regardless of small price movements in the stock.

## 3. Portfolio Variance and Correlation
Professional risk management requires understanding the correlation between assets.
- Negative Correlation: Assets that move in opposite directions (e.g., Gold vs. Stocks). Adding negatively correlated assets reduces total portfolio volatility.
- Beta Coefficient: Measures a stock's volatility relative to the overall market. A Beta > 1.0 means the stock is more volatile than the market.
