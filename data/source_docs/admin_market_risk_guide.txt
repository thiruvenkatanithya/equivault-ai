# ADMIN MARKET RISK AND CONTROL GUIDE

Original EquiVault learning material | Access level: Admin

## Purpose and boundaries

This guide describes a framework for reviewing market, portfolio, and process risks. It is not a trading mandate, a recommendation, or a substitute for an institution's approved policies, legal review, or independent risk oversight.

## Portfolio-level risk review

- Concentration: Measure exposure by issuer, sector, geography, currency, asset class, and common risk factor.
- Market sensitivity: Review how holdings may respond to broad market moves, interest-rate changes, currency shifts, and volatility shocks.
- Liquidity: Consider normal and stressed trading volume, bid-ask spreads, market depth, and the time needed to reduce a position.
- Leverage: Record gross and net exposure, borrowing terms, collateral requirements, and potential margin calls.
- Drawdown: Track peak-to-trough losses using a consistent time period and explain the assumptions.
- Counterparty and operational risk: Identify custody, settlement, valuation, data-quality, access-control, and business-continuity dependencies.

Historical volatility and scenario analysis describe possible risk; neither predicts or limits future losses.

## Scenario and stress testing

Use documented scenarios relevant to the portfolio, such as a broad equity decline, an interest-rate shock, currency depreciation, a liquidity contraction, or a correlated counterparty event. State which factors change, which remain fixed, the data date, and the limitations of the model.

Review both single-factor shocks and combined scenarios. A set of individually manageable shocks can create a larger combined impact when correlations change or liquidity disappears.

## Hedging governance

Before a hedge is considered, document the exposure being reduced, the instrument, basis risk, hedge horizon, liquidity, cost, collateral needs, and conditions for review or unwind. A hedge can introduce new risks and may not move in line with the exposure it is intended to offset.

Any hedge execution should follow authorized mandates, segregation of duties, approval limits, and an auditable process. This guide does not authorize a user or system to place trades.

## Controls and escalation

1. Assign an accountable owner to each material risk and define escalation thresholds in approved policy.
2. Verify market data provenance, timestamps, units, and stale-data handling.
3. Separate model inputs, model outputs, and human decisions in records.
4. Restrict access by role and review permissions periodically.
5. Record overrides, approvals, incidents, and remediation with a timestamp and rationale.
6. Escalate breaches, unreliable data, or model limitations to the designated risk owner.

## Review record checklist

- Portfolio and valuation date
- Data providers and timestamps
- Exposure and scenario definitions
- Assumptions, exclusions, and model version
- Results, limitations, and independent review
- Owner, approval, and follow-up action
